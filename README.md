👉 Ongoing project in Molecular Simulations and Drug Delivery Lab, IIT Roorkee

<u><b>Abstract of the work:</b></u>

The lattice-based "Ant-wall" model to study drug release from an excipient matrix (Singh et al., Physica A, 519, 98-108, 2019) is extended to the case of bilayered release formulations such as tablets or patches. The walls represent the polymeric excipient that is fixed in space. Ants represent drug molecules, which perform a random walk within the lattice. Unlike the previous study where the ants may be all "blind" (non-aggregating) or all "friendly" (aggregating) within a finite square lattice, here we consider a bilayered film containing "blind" and "friendly" layers with finite thickness but infinite length. We analyze the effects of the amount of walls (polymer), relative thickness of the layers, and lattice size on the drug release behavior. These provide useful guidance for the design of bilayered drug delivery systems.
